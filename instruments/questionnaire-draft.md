# Questionnaire — draft for review and pilot, not a deployed form
Start with the finalized consent information. Do not collect names, student IDs, employer names, or client identities.

## A. Eligibility and background
1. Do you consent? Yes / No (No ends survey).
2. Status: final-year IS student / IS graduate within past 1–3 years / industry practitioner / other.
3. Primary pathway: firm / freelance / hybrid / not working. Record current pathway; ask about first-entry experience separately if studying entry transition.
4. Months of relevant paid experience: numeric, nonnegative; optional.
5. Internship completed before first paid role? Yes / No / Not applicable.
6. Hours per week of self-directed tool learning before first role: numeric; optional.
7. Graduation year, if applicable. Confirm approved eligibility dates before launch.
Final-year students and industry practitioners should not complete graduate outcome comparisons. Industry practitioners may answer a separate requirements module; do not pool their responses as graduate observations.

## B. Tool categories
Repeat the following items for version control; containers; CI/CD; cloud deployment; development frameworks; agile project tracking.
Examples: Git/GitHub; Docker; continuous integration/deployment workflows; AWS/Azure; frameworks actually used locally; Jira/Trello. Examples are prompts, not verified local demand rankings.

For each category ask:
- Curriculum exposure: 1 none; 2 mentioned/demonstrated; 3 guided exercise; 4 independent coursework task; 5 repeated project use.
- Current proficiency: 1 cannot perform a basic task; 2 requires step-by-step guidance; 3 performs basic tasks with some help; 4 independently handles routine tasks; 5 handles complex tasks and assists others.
- Required proficiency in your current work: use the SAME proficiency anchors above.
- Workplace importance: 1 not important; 2 slightly; 3 moderately; 4 very; 5 critical.
- Add Not applicable / Cannot assess separately; encode as blank, not zero.
- Optional: describe a concrete task this tool is needed for, without identifying a client or employer.

Current proficiency versus current requirements measures current mismatch, not a retrospectively validated entry gap. If collecting entry ratings, use explicitly labeled separate entry-time items and acknowledge recall limitations.

## C. Common readiness items (graduates only)
Rate each for your first paid software role: 1 strongly disagree to 5 strongly agree; Not applicable / Cannot recall separately.
R1. I could complete routine assigned technical tasks without step-by-step assistance.
R2. I could manage shared code changes using the required workflow.
R3. I could prepare and deliver work in the required technical environment.
R4. I could plan and communicate task progress using the required collaboration workflow.
This proposed scale is unvalidated. Pilot and review its content and consistency before treating its mean as a readiness construct. Entry-time readiness and current tool proficiency use different time frames; interpret their association cautiously or revise the study to align them.

## D. Pathway-specific context (do not treat as interchangeable outcomes)
Firm: onboarding weeks; frequency of supervision; employer-provided training availability.
Freelance: contracts attempted/completed in a defined recall period; client feedback availability; access to mentors.
Set the same recall window for all respondents before deployment. Distinguish unavailable values from zero. Do not publish identifiable free text.

## E. Open response
What tool-related difficulty most affected your transition, and what university activity would have helped?
